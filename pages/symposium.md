## About

The Iowa State University Library's Digital Scholarship and Initiatives department and the Center for Excellence in the Arts and Humanities (CEAH), in collaboration with partners at Grinnell College and the University of Iowa, are excited to welcome you to our third annual Digital Scholarship Symposium, to be held March 25, 2026, in The Catalyst—Parks Library's dedicated digital research and learning space.

This symposium brings together faculty, students, librarians, digital practitioners, and community members to explore how digital methods, tools, and creative practices are transforming research, teaching, and public engagement. We welcome proposals from across Iowa's higher education institutions, organizations, and digital communities.

This year's theme, "Expanding Possibilities: Digital Scholarship for Communities, Classrooms, and Creative Futures" invites exploration of how digital scholarship bolsters student digital literacy and opens research to broader audiences beyond academic journals through digital platforms, interactive media, and open educational resources.

Registration has now closed. We look forward to seeing you!

### Panel 1—Public Digital Arts and Humanities: Community Impact Through Digital Projects

*10:00–11:15 a.m. \| The Catalyst*

**Moderator: Nikki White, University of Iowa**

#### Lessons Learned While Building Community Across State Lines

**Bambi Yost, Iowa State University**

In this session, I will share different ways that my students and I have worked with multiple nonprofit organizations, community groups, teachers and students, and school districts. Working with communities can be challenging even when they are located nearby. Digital tools (Adobe InDesign, Illustrator, Photoshop; AutoCAD, GIS, SketchUp, Revit, Lumion, and more) and video tools (predominantly ZOOM and Vimeo) enhance in-person and online communication leading to community owned master plans, design ideations, animated videos, and fundraising materials. A selection of these final products will be showcased.

Community-based projects with Philadelphia Public Schools, The Big Sandbox, Land Health Institute, New Hampshire Stormwater Center, Professional Landscape Architects, Virginia Beach School District, Chesapeake Bay Foundation, Architects, and local communities will be highlighted. Final products are shared online and have resulted in funding and several design-builds and full construction of these places. The process of working with numerous people across state lines will be shared and discussed. Lessons learned about what does and doesn't work well will be discussed.

#### Campus Cornerstones: A mapped history of Iowa State University

**Kevin Kane, Iowa State University GIS Facility, retired**

Launched in 2023, this research project investigates the planning and building history of the Iowa State University (ISU) campus through the lens of spatial humanities. Traditionally, historical archives—comprising maps, terrestrial photography, and narrative texts—exist in isolated silos, making it difficult to visualize the cohesive evolution of a physical environment. This presentation details a methodology for integrating these disparate datasets using QGIS, an open-source geographic information systems platform that serves as a bridge between static history and modern spatial data.

A critical milestone in this project was the 2024 partnership with the ISU Library's Digital Scholarship and Initiatives department. This collaboration facilitated the creation of a digital hub entitled "[Campus Cornerstones: A mapped history of Iowa State University](https://isu-digital.github.io/historic-buildings/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}" designed to share ongoing progress and findings with the broader community. By georeferencing historical maps and overlaying them with contemporary GIS layers and archival photographs, the project creates a high-fidelity chronological narrative of the campus's growth.

Attendees will learn about our team; Erin Ridnour, Digital Scholarship Librarian at the ISU Library, Mary Witkowski and Michaela Beck, Digital Scholarship and Initiatives Student Assistants, and myself; our research roles in the project; and how open-source tools can be leveraged to synthesize institutional memory, providing a blueprint for other organizations looking to transform archival records into interactive, spatial stories. This session highlights the power of collaborative digital scholarship in uncovering the hidden layers of our built environment.

#### Beyond the Museum Walls: University Museums and Public Digital Scholarship

**Caitlin Patton, Allison Sheridan; Iowa State University Museums**

In addition to three on-campus museums and the largest collection of Art on Campus of any university, the University Museums at Iowa State University uses digital tools to expand public access to its collections, support teaching and research, and foster community engagement beyond the museum walls. This panel presents a series of digital museum projects that make more than 30,000 collection objects accessible to global audiences, including the University Museums' [searchable database through eMuseum](https://emuseum.its.iastate.edu/collections){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, open-access [digital publications on PubPub](https://universitymuseums.pubpub.org/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, and a growing archive of recorded lectures and educational resources.

The goals of these initiatives are to increase public access to cultural heritage and enhance interdisciplinary learning across campus. Speakers will discuss the methods that support this work, with particular attention to object cataloging, archives, and publications, while employing various digital infrastructures to make the University Museums' collections more easily discovered. These back-end digital practices enhance curatorial initiatives and preservation goals, while supporting researchers and reducing the physical handling of fragile materials.

This presentation demonstrates how digital collections allow faculty, students, and the community to explore collections remotely and engage with materials that may otherwise be inaccessible. By integrating digital access with curatorial and educational practices, University Museums illustrate how museums can serve their missions in new and exciting ways to meet the needs of their contemporary audiences.

#### Critical Cataloging of Haitian Art

**Jane Mertens, Grinnell College**

The [Haitian Arts Digital Crossroad(HADC)](https://grinnell.emuseum.com/objects/images){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} project has worked digitize and make publicly available the large collection of Haitian Art held by the Waterloo Center for the Arts. As an information scientist I was brought on to the project to create descriptions and metadata for this collection. In this presentation I will talk about how I approached metadata for this collection. I will discuss what Critical Cataloging is, and how I applied it in this project. I will discuss how to build descriptions that bring the pieces into larger conversations while centering their unique cultural context. I used the Getty Thesaurus of Art and Architecture terms, built a material culture thesaurus, and built a Kreyol thesaurus. I will show how these metadata impact searching and description on the public facing website. I will then discuss what our next steps are as we worked to grow this digital repository of Haitian Art.

#### Campus Tree Map—Interactive Digital Record of Trees at Grinnell College

**Gabriela Roznawska, Grinnell College**

Grinnell Campus Tree Map is a project facilitated through the Vivero Digital Fellowship at Grinnell College. The project aims to create a community-oriented application with a map visualizing trees on Grinnell College grounds. At the current stage of development, users can filter display of different genus and species while accessing information about the environmental impact of each tree, details on different species, and general information about the project. The project is created using ArcGIS and ArcGIS Experience Builder.

With the festival having gone on for over 40 years and featured 600+ films, a new partnership between the Vivero Digital Fellows and Grinnell's Department of Alumni Relations has created an archival site dedicated to hosting the witty and ridiculous films from many years past. Under project-leads Tierney Steelberg and Adam Kelley-Chown, Kiera Rennick has revamped and updated the [informational](https://titularhead.sites.grinnell.edu/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and [archival](https://database.titularhead.sites.grinnell.edu/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} sites, as well as working alongside Kelley-Chown to host a "Virtual TitHead" event connecting alumni of the college (some in the film industry) to student filmmakers.

#### Building a Digital Home for Grinnell XCTF

**James Snyder, Grinnell College**

I built the Grinnell College Cross Country and Track & Field website ([xctf.sites.grinnell.edu](http://xctf.sites.grinnell.edu/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}) to unify the program's culture into a single, public digital home. Developed with WordPress and Elementor Pro, the platform functions as both a communications hub and a living archive—bringing together alumni spotlights, athlete-written stories, newsletters, recruiting resources, and student research on running and sports science. Content that once disappeared into social media or email inboxes is now permanent, searchable, and accessible worldwide. In this lightning talk, I'll share the tools, design strategy, and solo workflow behind the project and argue that lightweight, student-built platforms can act as powerful digital scholarship—preserving community memory while actively strengthening connection, visibility, and belonging.

#### Inclusive Communications for Student Organizations

**Valery Mayorga Garcia, Grinnell College**

The project "Inclusive Communications for Student Organizations" aims to create digital accessibility resources designed specifically for student organizations, helping student organization leaders host events, share information, and communicate in ways that are inclusive of all participants. In this project, I research and develop easy-to-use guides and templates for various digital platforms and communications. In addition, I collaborate with campus partners to ensure that accessibility is built into student-led organizations and activities. The goal of this project is to empower student leaders with the knowledge and tools they need to reduce barriers and foster a culture of access within their organizations.

#### Conducting Accessibility Audits in Digital History Projects

**Morgan Smith, Grinnell College**

This presentation discusses the Laetitia Legacy Project, which explores the life of early female Grinnell Sociologist Laetitia Moon Conard (1871–1946). Laetitia earned a PhD from the University of Chicago in 1899, and taught sociology at Grinnell as an unpaid lecturer for 15 years. She was also deeply committed to social activism, often said to embody "internationalism, pacifism, socialism, [and] humanitarianism." The digital site for the project mirrors the private/public divide of women's lives that is often used by feminist scholars and historians. This presentation will take a process-based approach to exploring how to conduct an accessibility audit during the final stages of a project's life before a launch.

#### Presenting Chinese Early Alumni at Grinnell College

**Chikako Inoue, Grinnell College**

In this lightening talk, I will introduce a [web page](https://chinesealumni.sites.grinnell.edu/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} that I help establishing to present Professor Feng's research on Chinese alumni in the early 20th century at Grinnell College. First, I will briefly give an overview of the research, including its value, potential audience, and the research outcome so far. Second, I will explore presentation technique using digital tools such as ArcGIS, HTML, and WordPress. Finally, I will address to the further work to be done and invite feedback.

#### Mapping Racial Trauma in Schools

**Karolina Calleros, Grinnell College, Vivero Digital Fellows Program**

Mapping Racial Trauma in Schools is a project created by Dr. Jones who seeks to understand and explore racialized trauma and curriculum violence within schools across the United States. The project documents and visually maps the incidents of racialized violence including curriculum violence, physical, racially offensive acts, verbal or written racialized comments, threats, or intimidation. We help visually map out the incidents using ArcGIS software, so students, parents, educators, and communities can see where and how frequently these incidents occur. This visual approach of the map helps shift racial trauma from isolated experiences to collective, systemic realities that demand accountability and action. The platform serves as both an educational resource and a call to action, advocate for change, recognize trends and push schools and districts to address institutional failures that can compromise student safety and learning.

My role in the project focuses on managing and refining the website to ensure that its purpose is clear and accessible to a broad public audience. This includes supporting user engagement, clarifying definitions we use on the website, and strengthening the site's function as a community-centered educational tool. Ultimately, Mapping Racial Trauma in Schools works to foster awareness, accountability, and safer learning environments, through collaborative work across the United States.

### Panel 2—Digital Pedagogy: Expanding Digital Literacy and Creativity in the Classroom

*1:45–3:00 p.m. \| The Catalyst*

**Moderator: Donna Brooks, University of Iowa**

#### Demonstrating Four Academic Uses of the Fortepan IA Photo History Platform

Fortepan IA, a photo history platform featuring photographs of Iowa everyday life gathered from family albums, newspaper archives, and small museums, launched in 2015 a the University of Northern Iowa with 2000 photographs. Today, with over 26,000 high resolution photographs, close to 8,000 tags (all searchable via Google Image search), and a growing representation of small town life across the state, the platform has become an increasingly valuable tool for academic research and pedagogy.

By automatically organizing these images by time, place, and theme (tag), the platform powerfully visualizes all aspects of Iowa history. When a user searches for a tag like "hairstyle," a chronology of hairstyles appears immediately before them in both grid and timeline view. Search across multiple terms and locations and the platform reveals undiscovered themes about Midwest life in the 19th and 20th centuries. A new map view allows a viewer to visualize geolocated photographs on a map. The platform's built-in interpretation tools (FotoAlbum, FotoStory, FotoSphere Tour) and display and embedding capabilities offer ripe primary resources for student projects and digital humanities research. And, easy access to such an array of searchable, non-AI-touched photographs that capture human lived experiences – all immediately downloadable with a CC-BY-SA Creative Commons license – is a treasure trove for artists, community members, storytellers, senior centers, and local historians.

Our panel features four ways academics can use (and are already using) the public platform.

#### New Fortepan IA Platform Features

**Bettina Fabos, Ph.D., co-Director of Fortepan US; Director of Fortepan IA; Professor Emeritus in Interactive Digital Studies, the University of Northern Iowa**

Fabos will unveil the platform's newest Map view feature and demonstrate how academics can fortify their humanities research and creative projects through Fortepan IA. She will also explain a pathway for adopting the open source platform code to create a customized research tool to both visualize and interpret a specific line of research.

#### Using the Fortepan IA Photo HIstory Platform of everyday Life to examine Midwest themes in Women and Gender Studies

**Paige Hibbs, Graduate Student in Women and Gender Studies, University of Northern Iowa; Tara Wilson-Pickering: Graduate Student in Women and Gender Studies, University of Northern Iowa**

UNI Graduate Students Paige Hibbs and Tara Wilson-Pickering will present a number of ways they are using the platform to examine rural midwest life through the academic lens of gender. By applying a customized set of new research-related tags to existing images related to gender (e.g., gender performance, cross-dressing) and using these filters to organize themes for analysis, they are eliciting fascinating conclusions about gender roles in Iowa everyday life.

#### Hands on way to teach Public History

**Kevin Mason, Ph.D., Department of History, University of Northern Iowa**

Dr. Kevin Mason will explore how integrating Fortepan IA into an undergraduate public history course can anchor a sustained, collaborative campus history project. Building on Fortepan's open, timeline-based interface and emphasis on vernacular photographs, the course treats students not simply as users of a digital archive but as co-creators of a public campus memory project. In this project, students produce a Fortepan-inspired fotostory that braids together images from Fortepan Iowa, newly digitized campus photographs, and short interpretive texts aimed at public audiences as a part of a broader interpretive project. The Fortepan project serves as a scaffold for collaborative public history practice, challenging students to integrate the practical skills and technological possibilities of Fortepan into a broader interpretive context that includes other creative work including interpretive panel design, website construction, varied forms of writing, and live interpretation.

#### Hands on way to teach the History of Iowa

**Brian Rumsey, Ph.D., Department of History, University of Northern Iowa**

Professor of History Dr. Brian Rumsey will explain how he is using Fortepan IA to connect his students' own personal family histories to the history of Iowa. Since most of his students have grown up in the state, he is asking them to actively participate in the platform: They are bringing in their own family's photo albums; curating the most meaningful photos; learning how to scan and upload, and add tags. learning digital literacy scanning procedures in the process), analyzing their family photos in juxtaposition with other images in the platform, and connect into the larger narratives of the state, country, and world.

## Call for Proposals

The Iowa State University Library's Digital Scholarship and Initiatives department and the Center for Excellence in the Arts and Humanities (CEAH), in collaboration with partners at Grinnell College and the University of Iowa, invite proposals for the third annual Digital Scholarship Symposium, to be held Wednesday, March 25, 2026, in The Catalyst—Parks Library's dedicated digital research and learning space.

This symposium brings together faculty, students, librarians, digital scholarship practitioners, and community members to explore how digital methods and tools are transforming research, teaching, art, and public engagement. We welcome proposals from across Iowa's higher education institutions and the broader creative and scholarly community.

The Symposium will feature two panel sessions and a series of lightning talks focused on digital scholarship projects and initiatives.

### Panel Sessions—Call for Panelists

Panelists will offer a 15-minute presentation on their work and process, followed by questions and discussion with fellow panelists. Panel proposals are open to faculty, staff, and graduate students, and may include multiple presenters.

#### Panel 1—Public Digital Arts and Humanities: Community Impact through Digital Projects

We seek panelists whose work demonstrates how digital projects connect research with public audiences, amplify community stories, or foster collaborative knowledge creation. Relevant areas include (but are not limited to):

- Digital storytelling, oral history initiatives, or community archives
- Public-facing digital humanities or arts projects
- Interactive experiences such as mapping, VR/AR, exhibitions, or digital installations
- Partnerships with local organizations, libraries, museums, or community groups

#### Panel 2—Digital Pedagogy: Expanding Digital Literacy and Creativity in the Classroom

We invite educators and practitioners to share teaching practices that foster creativity, advance digital literacy, encourage multimodal expression, and prioritize critical engagement with technology. Topics may include (but are not limited to):

- Innovative digital assignments or multimodal student projects
- Classroom integration of data visualization, 3D modeling, text analysis, podcasting/audio narrative, or other digital methods
- Assessment of digital literacy and student engagement

### Lightning Talks—Call for Student Presenters

Lightning talks are three to five-minute, high-energy presentations designed to showcase the digital scholarship-related work of undergraduate and graduate students. Lightning talk proposals may represent group projects but should designate a single presenter.

#### Lightning Talks

Topics may include (but are not limited to):

- Prototype tools, platforms, or digital methods
- Early-stage research employing digital or data-driven methods
- New collaborations or pitches for digital projects
- Experimental or speculative use of digital media or technologies

### Proposal Guidelines

We invite applicants to submit proposals through the [Symposium Proposal Submission Form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSeGsxL8sKEb_9SEMIYdDEnHh0PmHIs9k0qEHQ1sa9ECLawgOA/viewform){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} by February 2. Submissions should include:

- Presenter name(s), affiliation(s), and contact information
- Session type: Panel 1, Panel 2, or Lightning Talk
- Title of Presentation
- Abstract
- Panel abstracts (150–250 words) should describe the project, its goals, methods, and relevance to digital scholarship
- Lightning talk abstracts (75–125 words) should offer a concise snapshot of the project and outline what the presenter intends to explore
- Brief bio (50–100 words) for each presenter
- Technical needs (if applicable)

### Submission Deadline

Proposals are accepted January 5, 2026 through 11:59 p.m. on February 2, 2026. Respondents will be notified of accepted proposals by February 9, 2026.
